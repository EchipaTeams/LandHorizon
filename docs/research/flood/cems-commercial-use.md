# Do the Copernicus data policy and the CEMS / GFM terms allow GFM layers, and products derived from them, to be used in a commercial product?
Date: 2026-11-23
Scope: In: EU legal basis (Delegated Regulation 1159/2013, Regulation 2021/696), the CEMS Terms and Conditions, GFM catalogue metadata, official statements, attribution string. Out: other datasets' licences (WorldCover, EGMS, JRC hazard maps, ERA5), flood methods. This is a reading of the texts by a researcher, not legal advice.

The article text as retrieved has no purpose limit and no mention of commercial use or of a ban on it.

## Relevance to LandHorizon
**Does the text allow a commercial product? (sourced, then inference)**
- Sourced: the law and the CEMS terms grant a free, worldwide right to reproduce, distribute, communicate, adapt, modify and combine, with no purpose limit and no wording that excludes commercial use (claims 1, 3, 4, 8, 10). A derived raster is "adaptation, modification and combination", and the IP in the adaptation belongs to its creators (claim 12).
- Inference: nothing in the texts I could read excludes commercial use, so a commercial product that reads GFM from the open EODC catalogue and serves a derived raster fits the stated grant. This is a reading of silence, not an explicit permission. The terms do not say "commercial use is allowed". Do not write that in the report. Say "free to reuse, adapt and combine under the CEMS terms".
- Sourced and unlike CLMS: no CEMS sentence confirming commercial use was found (claim 18 is unverified).

**Conditions that apply (sourced)**
- Attribution when distributing or communicating to the public: "Generated using Copernicus Emergency Management Service information [Year]", or the modified variant (claims 5, 11).
- No implication of EU endorsement (claim 5, Art. 8 of 1159/2013).
- Say clearly the data was adapted or modified (claims 5, 11).
- Information only, not an early warning, as is, no warranty, damage claims against the EU and providers waived (claims 6, 13). Third-party users of our product are not covered by that waiver. Our own report disclaimer must carry the same limits (inference).
- Terms can change; the PDF is dated 2023-06-07 (claim 14).

**What the report and README must say (proposal, partly inference)**
- Report footer or map credit: "Contains modified Copernicus Emergency Management Service information 2026" (our raster is a per-pixel count, so "modified" applies; use the year of the processed data or of publication, the terms leave it open).
- One line: flood history is derived from Copernicus GFM; screening indicator only; not an early warning; no official endorsement by the EU or Copernicus; provided as is.
- README: link to the CEMS terms, the attribution string, and the licence "other (CEMS terms)", not CC BY.

**Pipeline, cost, compute, coverage:** none changed by this finding. Free access and Galati coverage depend on GFM, not on the licence. A product use does not need registration for the open catalogue (claim 14, medium).

## Review
Reviewer: independent verifier, 2026-11-23. I read the CEMS PDF in full (3 pages, rendered). I read the EUR-Lex texts and the STAC JSON through a fetch tool that returned verbatim quotes. So "verified" for the EU legal texts rests on quoted excerpts, not on reading the whole page myself.

### Verdicts
1. `verified`: Art. 53(1)(a) text matches word for word and goes on "...all Copernicus data and Copernicus information and combine them with other data and information". The word "commercial" does not appear in Art. 53.
2. `verified`: the intro sentence matches. Minor point: Art. 53(1)(b) lists **five** limitations, and the claim leaves out (v), "protection of reliable access to Copernicus data and Copernicus information for European users". "Where 1159/2013 stays relevant" is an inference. 1159/2013 was adopted under Regulation 911/2010, not under Art. 53(2). EUR-Lex shows it as in force, and I found no successor delegated act.
3. `verified`: the Art. 3 quote and the Art. 2(b) definition are both present.
4. `verified`: the Art. 7 quote is present, and so is "for the purpose of the following use in so far as it is lawful:". The text never uses the word "commercial".
5. `verified`: the Art. 8 quote is present (straight vs curly apostrophe only), and so is the "adapted or modified ... clearly state this" sentence.
6. `verified`: the Art. 9 quote and the Art. 4 "Free access shall be given..." are present.
7. `verified`: the Art. 12 quote is present. Art. 11 covers international agreements and IP. The claim is hedged correctly as low confidence.
8. `verified`: the quote is exact in the PDF. Source date: "07/06/2023" is ambiguous. The PDF filename (`20230706_...`) suggests 6 July 2023, not 7 June. The fetch tool also reported PDF file metadata of 23 July 2024. Write the date as "Last modified 07/06/2023 (as printed)".
9. `verified`: the quote is exact. The caveat is accurate: the "Access and use" grant names only "the data of CEMS EFAS & GloFAS", and GFM is listed as "including" under those systems.
10. `verified`: (d) is exact. I read all 3 pages and "commercial" does not appear. The grant is also "Subject to their compliance with the terms" and "within the limits allowed under applicable law", which is not quite "no purpose limit". It is a lawful-use limit, not a purpose limit.
11. `verified`: both notices are exact and "following or similar notice" is present. The year is undefined, as the researcher says.
12. `verified`: exact. The same paragraph also says use "shall not confer any intellectual property rights or other proprietary right to the user" over the original data.
13. `verified`: both quotes are exact.
14. `verified`: all three statements are exact in the PDF. The note that GFM is not behind registration is unverified (see the relevance issues below).
15. `verified`: `"license":"other"`, the link title and the href match. Providers: JRC CEMS (licensor), EODC (processor, host).
16. `verified`: the sci:citation string and DOI 10.1109/IGARSS47720.2021.9554214 match. JRC CEMS is listed with the role "licensor".
17. `unsupported`: the quote is only the date, which is present, so it doesn't support the substance of the claim. The page has **no FAQ**, so the "FAQ says ... open basis" part is not backed by this source. The page also points to separate "Citation Guidelines" rather than reproducing the EWDS notice, so "same text" is only partly shown. On-demand Mapping is also a different CEMS component from GFM.
18. `unsupported`: no quote was given. I opened the page myself. It does say "Yes, you are allowed to use CLMS data for all purposes, including commercial." So the CLMS half is true and can now be cited with that quote. The CEMS half (no equivalent sentence) is a negative finding, and my own search found none either.

Count: 16 verified, 2 unsupported, 0 misquoted, 0 unreachable.

### What the researcher missed
- **A readable, current HTML copy of the same terms exists**: "Terms of use of the CEMS Early Warning Data Store (rev. 11)". It shows the same "Last modified: 07/06/2023", names "the Global Flood Monitoring product", has the same grant and notices, and does not contain "commercial". Cite it in the README instead of the object-store PDF and the unreadable React page. https://ewds.climate.copernicus.eu/licences/terms-of-use-cems
- **The PUM's CC BY 4.0 statement probably covers the manual, not the data.** Search snippets of the 2023 PUM read "the reuse of this document is authorised under ... CC BY 4.0". I could not render the PDF, so this is unconfirmed, but it means the CC BY lead in the open questions should not be read as a data licence. https://elib.dlr.de/196316/1/gfm_pum_v20231005_compressed.pdf
- **Legal basis of 1159/2013**: it was adopted under Regulation 911/2010. No delegated act under Art. 53(2) of 2021/696 was found, and EUR-Lex lists 1159/2013 as in force. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2013/1159/oj
- **The fifth Art. 53(1)(b) limitation** (reliable access for European users) is not listed in claim 2. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2021/696/oj

### Problems in "Relevance to LandHorizon"
- "Sourced and unlike CLMS: ..." is labelled sourced, but it rests on claim 18, which is unsupported as filed. Re-cite it with the CLMS quote above. https://land.copernicus.eu/en/faq/data-use-terms-and-conditions
- "A product use does not need registration for the open catalogue (claim 14, medium)" is not backed by a verified claim. Claim 14 only says that *some* CEMS data is restricted. Nothing shows that GFM is in the unrestricted set, or that the STAC assets (not only the collection JSON) can be read without login. Mark it as an open question.
- "Third-party users of our product are not covered by that waiver" is placed under "Conditions that apply (sourced)" but is inference. It is tagged only at the end of the bullet.
- "no purpose limit" (in the relevance section and claim 10): the grant is limited to use "within the limits allowed under applicable law". Say "no stated purpose limit beyond lawful use".
- Missing, given the constraints: constraints.md says the GEE free tier is **non-commercial**. The fallback flood pipeline (`pipeline/flood_earthengine.py`) runs on GEE. If LandHorizon is pitched as commercial, that compute route needs the same scrutiny, even though GFM via EODC is fine. The scope excludes other datasets but not compute terms, and "Pipeline, cost, compute ... none changed" overlooks this. Source: docs/research/constraints.md (Data and money).
- The recommended wording is sound and respects the constraints: "free to reuse, adapt and combine under the CEMS terms", "not an early warning", and the screening-indicator framing (no claim of explicit commercial permission). Keep the helpdesk question open.
